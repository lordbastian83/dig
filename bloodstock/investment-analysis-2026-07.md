The core finding is that the 2026 middle market is running hot — the Tattersalls
July Sale median jumped **36% year-on-year to 15,000 gns** (average +17% to
30,015 gns, 88% clearance) — which compresses expected ROI on fashionable
yearlings. The racing-economics edge currently sits in the **dual-purpose
conversion trade**: buying proven Flat form at horses-in-training sales and
re-routing to juvenile/novice hurdles, the profile James Owen has monetised at an
elite level (East India Dock: dominant Triumph Trial winner, Grade-2 class as a
juvenile; Rogue Diplomat: 2026 Royal Hunt Cup winner from a 28-runner field).

## Pillar analysis

### Target 1 — HIT conversion profile (PRIMARY)

| Pillar | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Pedigree & genetics | Require a proven middle-distance sire line with stamina carry (dosage skewed Solid/Professional); dam side with winners at 10f+. Stamina is the transferable asset Flat markets under-price and jumps markets pay for. |
| Performance | Official rating **80–95**, form at 10–14f, aged 3 (or unexposed 4), still progressive on the figures. Below 80 the ceiling is too low; above 95 the Flat market re-prices the horse past our EV cap. |
| Physical & veterinary | Sound, fluent action; **no wind-surgery history**; scope/wind certificate reviewed pre-bid. If certificates or repository data are unavailable at inspection, the mandatory −20% valuation discount applies. |
| Ecosystem synergy | This is the exact James Owen production line (East India Dock, Burdett Road cohort). A 17%-SR dual-purpose yard converts this profile at above-market rates, and a winning juvenile hurdler is a liquid resale asset. |

### Target 2 — Premier Sale speed yearling (OPPORTUNISTIC)

| Pillar | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Pedigree & genetics | Proven 2yo sire (≥10% 2yo winners-to-runners) in the £30–50k yearling-median tier; ≥1 stakes horse in the first two dams; dam a winner at 2. |
| Performance | Unraced — inference only from pedigree precocity and physical scores. This absence of speed figures is itself a risk input. |
| Physical & veterinary | Jan–Mar foal, A/B walk grade, correct forelimbs, above-par projected stride. Goffs repository X-rays must be clean; if unavailable, mandatory −20% discount. |
| Ecosystem synergy | Harry's 750 eligibility (all 445 lots) adds a real, quantifiable bonus-EV layer; a sharp Owen-trained 2yo winner is an exportable asset. But the bonus structure is already priced into a rising Doncaster market. |

<quantitative_reasoning>

**Common assumptions.** Syndicate hurdle rate: 15% project margin. GBP↔gns:
1 gn = £1.05. Mandatory rule: any missing critical veterinary/speed data ⇒
apply −20% to the computed valuation.

**Target 1 — HIT conversion (12-month horizon to end of juvenile-hurdle season)**

Operating cost, 12 months (NH training, entries, transport, 3% insurance):
**£28,000**.

Expected inflows:
- Prize money EV over ~5 starts at a 17%-SR yard with juvenile-hurdle
  overperformance: **£9,000**.
- Residual value tree (probability × outcome):
  - 15% Grade-1/2-class juvenile → £250,000 → £37,500
  - 30% wins juvenile/novice hurdles → £80,000 → £24,000
  - 35% moderate hurdler → £25,000 → £8,750
  - 20% fails to transfer / injury → £5,000 → £1,000
  - E[residual] = **£71,250**
- Total E[inflows] = **£80,250**

Breakeven purchase cap = 80,250 − 28,000 = **£52,250**
Margin-adjusted cap = 52,250 / 1.15 = **£45,435** ≈ **42,000 gns** (after 5%
buyer frictions, rounded down — hard limit).
If vet documentation incomplete at fall of hammer: 42,000 × 0.8 = **34,000 gns**.

Cross-check vs market: the July Sale median of 15,000 gns and the 80–95-rated
stayer trade band (typically 40–80k gns in a rising market) mean the limit is
biddable but will lose the fashionable lots. That is the discipline working,
not failing.

**Target 2 — Premier yearling (14-month horizon to end of 2yo season)**

Market baseline: 2025 Premier median £35,000; apply +10% 2026 market drift
(conservative vs the +36% July Sale signal) → adjusted baseline **£38,500**.
Top-quartile spec premium ≈ +0.5σ (σ_mid ≈ £22,000) → indicated market price
≈ **£49,500**. That is what the ring will charge; EV must justify it.

Operating cost, 14 months (frictions, breaking, 11 months training, entries,
insurance): **£36,000**.

Expected inflows:
- Base 2yo prize EV (~4 starts): **£7,500**
- Harry's 750 bonus EV: P(win a £25k bonus maiden) ≈ 0.12 → £3,000; Harry's
  Half Million participation/placement EV ≈ £2,500 → **£5,500**
- Residual tree:
  - 12% rated 90+ precocious winner → £180,000 → £21,600
  - 18% maiden/nursery winner, 80–89 → £60,000 → £10,800
  - 32% placed, 65–79 → £15,000 → £4,800
  - 28% below 65 → £5,000 → £1,400
  - 10% wastage → £0
  - E[residual] = **£38,600**
- Total E[inflows] = **£51,600**

Breakeven purchase cap = 51,600 − 36,000 = **£15,600**
Margin-adjusted cap = 15,600 / 1.15 = **£13,565** → hard limit **£13,500**
(repository must be clean; if not, £10,800).

Conclusion: the EV cap (£13,500) sits far below the indicated market price
(~£49,500). **The median-market Doncaster yearling cannot be justified to
stakeholders on racing economics in this cycle.** Participation is
opportunistic only — late-session value at or under the cap — or pass.

**Sensitivity.** Target 1 stays EV-positive up to 52,000 gns at breakeven; the
15% margin is the buffer. Target 2 would need E[residual] ≈ £74,000 (nearly
double the modelled tree) to justify the market median — that is hope, not a
model.

</quantitative_reasoning>

1. **Market heat** — July Sale median +36% YoY; the Autumn HIT sale will be

2. **Missing-data rule** — any lot without complete vet/wind documentation is

3. **Single-asset concentration** — one horse is one draw from the outcome
   tree. The syndicate structure must disclose the 20% fail branch.
4. **Yearling route** — explicitly not recommended at market median this
   cycle; revisit if Doncaster trade softens below £30,000 median on Day 1.
